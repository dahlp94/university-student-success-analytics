# Institutional Brief: Six-Year Graduation Outcomes

**University of Arkansas | 2024 IPEDS Data**

## Overview

This analysis compares six-year bachelor's graduation rates at the University of Arkansas with five peer universities. It uses IPEDS data for students who entered in 2018 and examines whether differences in financial-aid composition affect institutional comparisons.

## Key Findings

Arkansas had an observed six-year graduation rate of **70.52%**, compared with **70.87%** across the five peers combined.

After adjusting Arkansas's graduation rate to match the peers' pooled financial-aid composition, its rate decreased to **69.31%**.

| Measure | Rate |
|---|---:|
| Arkansas observed | 70.52% |
| Arkansas peer-adjusted | 69.31% |
| Five-peer pooled benchmark | 70.87% |
| Observed peer gap | −0.35 pp |
| Adjusted peer gap | −1.56 pp |

## Interpretation

Arkansas had a smaller proportion of Pell recipients and a larger proportion of students receiving neither Pell grants nor subsidized loans than its peers.

Under the common financial-aid distribution, Arkansas's graduation rate was 1.21 percentage points lower than its observed rate. This suggests that financial-aid composition matters when comparing overall graduation outcomes.

## Limitations and Next Steps

These comparisons are descriptive, not causal. Financial-aid categories also do not fully capture students' socioeconomic backgrounds or academic preparation.

The next phase will examine multiple entering cohorts to determine how changes in financial-aid composition and within-group graduation rates contributed to changes in overall outcomes over time.
